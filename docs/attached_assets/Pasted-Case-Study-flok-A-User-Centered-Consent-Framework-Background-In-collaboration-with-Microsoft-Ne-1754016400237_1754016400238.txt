Case Study: flok - A User-Centered Consent Framework
Background
In collaboration with Microsoft New England Research and Development, my team at flok set out to design an ethical and transparent digital consent framework tailored for health data sharing. This project was part of Microsoft Research’s Project Resolve, dedicated to building health equity and social justice through collaborative, community-driven innovation.
flok is a nonprofit supporting a community of individuals with metabolic disorders who must closely monitor dietary intake and health data. Recognizing the research value of this sensitive information, flok aimed to ensure community members maintained clear, informed control over their personal health data before sharing it with third-party researchers.
Problem
In today's digital landscape, consent often boils down to a quick, thoughtless click, which is insufficient for sensitive health data. flok’s community, vulnerable to privacy risks due to its small size, required a dynamic, clear, and reversible consent mechanism that went beyond mere legal compliance.
Our Approach
We began by dissecting what consent meant in various contexts—bodily, legal, and digital. Inspired by Planned Parenthood’s "FRIES" (Freely given, Reversible, Informed, Enthusiastic, Specific) consent guidelines, we crafted "FAIR" (Freely given, Active, Informed, Reversible) guidelines tailored specifically for digital health data. Unlike bodily consent, digital consent is ongoing, thus necessitating robust mechanisms for continuous data control and revocation.
Research & Insights
Through extensive focus groups and co-design sessions with flok community members, key insights emerged:
Community members deeply value research on metabolic disorders but prioritize having explicit control over their data.
Existing consent models, such as the "Meta Consent" approach proposed by Ploug and Holm, were seen as overly complex.
Community members strongly preferred an intuitive, personalized consent process.
Child assent was an essential consideration, requiring tiered engagement based on the child's cognitive ability.
Design Solution
Leveraging these insights, we designed and prototyped an intuitive consent interface for the flok app, featuring:
Clear delineation between required and optional data, allowing granular control.
Simple interfaces explaining why certain data points were requested.
Integrated child assent mechanisms through a tiered system:
No Child Assent – For young children or limited cognitive abilities.
Basic Assent – Awareness-building video on data privacy.
Full Participation – Complete consent process mirroring caregiver involvement.
The consent was fully reversible, allowing community members to withdraw and request data deletion at any point, even post-study, maintaining complete transparency about data usage and research outcomes.
Technical Implementation
We proposed a robust backend data architecture using Azure cloud services, ensuring a secure and responsive system aligned with our design specifications. Consent decisions made in the user interface directly triggered backend actions, such as securely deleting user data upon consent revocation, ensuring user decisions were effectively enforced.
Results & Impact
Our iterative co-design approach resulted in overwhelmingly positive feedback from the flok community. Members appreciated the simplicity, transparency, and empowerment provided by the new consent framework.
The final deliverables included comprehensive design specifications and backend technical requirements, enabling seamless integration into the flok app.
Next Steps
Microsoft Research will publish our methodology and findings as part of Project Resolve, exploring broader community applications and further refinement of our innovative consent framework to foster ethical health data practices across digital platforms.
