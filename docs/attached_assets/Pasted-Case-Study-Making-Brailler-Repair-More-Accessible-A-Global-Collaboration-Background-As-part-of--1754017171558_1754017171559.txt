Case Study: Making Brailler Repair More Accessible – A Global Collaboration
Background
As part of my Technology, Accessibility, and Design (TAD) class at Olin College, our team collaborated with the Perkins School for the Blind to make the process of cleaning and repairing Braillers more accessible. Braillers—mechanical devices used by people who are blind to write in Braille—require periodic maintenance, which is often handled by a small, dedicated network of repair technicians.
Our work focused on two primary goals:
Making Brailler repair and cleaning processes more accessible for blind technicians and those in resource-limited environments.
Exploring how the distribution of tools, parts, and knowledge could be improved to support technicians worldwide.
The Problem
The current Brailler repair ecosystem is built on informal networks of technicians, with inconsistent access to information, tools, and replacement parts. Blind technicians, in particular, face a lack of accessible training resources and tailored support. International technicians often struggle to get parts due to shipping delays, credit card restrictions, or limited funding. This results in many Braillers sitting unusable for months or even years.
Research & Insights
We interviewed 11 technicians across the U.S., Canada, Australia, and Kenya. These conversations highlighted several key insights:
Knowledge Gaps & Decentralization: Much of the repair knowledge is passed down informally. Without access to Perkins trainings or personal networks, technicians often feel disconnected.
Innovation in Isolation: Technicians develop their own tools and techniques, particularly in remote or under-resourced areas.
Accessibility Barriers: While a few blind technicians repair Braillers, most find the process inaccessible due to the lack of tactile guides, accessible documentation, or specialized training.
Supply Chain Disparities: Technicians outside the U.S. struggle to obtain parts—some reported waiting years for a single shipment.
Our Solution
We proposed a set of recommendations to Perkins aimed at increasing accessibility, connection, and sustainability within the Brailler repair community:
Digital, Modular Repair Manual: A redesigned, screenreader-accessible manual that walks through common repair tasks using a mix of text, CAD animations, and voiceover. This would make learning repair skills possible for blind users and newcomers.
Community Forum for Technicians: A centralized platform for sharing tips, tools, and repair stories—modeled after Discourse forums—to build community knowledge and improve communication across regions.
Updated Technician Directory: A more dynamic, user-updatable listing of active technicians, improving access for Brailler users and allowing for timely contact.
Improved International Parts Access: Recommendations included accepting foreign credit cards and revisiting bulk purchasing and distribution strategies, especially in underfunded regions.
Impact
Our work uncovered both the resilience and challenges of the global Brailler repair community. We highlighted pathways to amplify existing ingenuity while connecting isolated technicians and blind users to the tools and knowledge they need. By sharing community-driven adaptations—like tactile tools, kerosene cleaning alternatives, and custom repair jigs—we helped surface grassroots innovation that could be more widely shared.
Reflections & Growth
This project taught me how deeply access to tools and knowledge affects global equity in assistive technology. Through interviews, research synthesis, and accessible design thinking, I learned to listen to and advocate for marginalized voices within a technical system. Most of all, I saw the power of community-led solutions and the role designers can play in connecting and supporting those networks.
Looking Ahead
We hope Perkins and similar organizations will build on these insights to foster a more inclusive and connected technician community. By improving infrastructure, accessibility, and communication, Braillers can remain reliable tools of independence for users around the world.
