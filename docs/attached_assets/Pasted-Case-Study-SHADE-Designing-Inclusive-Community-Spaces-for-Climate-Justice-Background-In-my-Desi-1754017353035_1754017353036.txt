Case Study: SHADE - Designing Inclusive Community Spaces for Climate Justice
Background
In my Design Resources for Empowerment and Making (DREAM) class, our team collaborated with Cambridge's "Shade is Social Justice" initiative, aimed at addressing heat inequities exacerbated by climate change. Recognizing that heat risk disproportionately affects certain neighborhoods, our goal was to develop shade structures that provided physical relief, promoted community interaction, and created inclusive spaces specifically designed for teenagers.
The Problem
Average summer temperatures in Cambridge are projected to increase significantly, exacerbating existing inequalities in heat exposure. Furthermore, teenagers often lack dedicated community spaces where they feel welcomed, resulting in feelings of exclusion or perceptions of "loitering." Without inclusive spaces, teenagers may seek out alternative places that potentially expose them to negative influences.
Research & Insights
Collaborating closely with community stakeholders and external partners, such as MIRROR, Building Ways, Citizens of the World, Equity Roadmap, Olin College, and Pneuhaus, we identified critical needs:
Creating welcoming spaces that specifically appeal to teenagers, fostering a sense of belonging and inclusion.
Flexible spatial arrangements to accommodate diverse social activities and interactions.
Interactive, engaging features that resonate with teenagers, encouraging sustained use and positive community involvement.
Our Solution
We developed two complementary solutions: Soft Walls and Solid Walls, each addressing specific community needs and focusing on creating an inviting environment for teenagers.
Soft Walls
Flexible Design: Soft, layered fabric walls that adjust easily, creating dynamic spaces adaptable for socializing, events, and relaxation.
Sustainable & Vibrant Materials: Incorporating natural dyes and patchwork to ensure visual appeal, environmental sustainability, and youth-friendly aesthetics.
Integration & Movement: Engineered for vertical movement, using tensioned side mounts and pulley systems for effortless repositioning.
Solid Walls
Interactive Engagement: Mobile walls designed with both vertical and lateral movements, enhancing interaction and customization for teenagers.
Can Wall Prototype: A weatherproof, visually engaging wall created from recycled cans, promoting sustainability, creativity, and a sense of ownership among teen users.
Textured Rotational Panels: Panels capable of rotation and horizontal movement, themed by color, adding sensory and interactive dimensions specifically appealing to young community members.
Impact
Our shade structures were positively received for their innovative approach to creating youth-centric, climate-responsive community spaces. By prioritizing inclusivity and interactive features, teenagers gained a dedicated space where they felt welcomed and valued, reducing the likelihood of seeking alternative, potentially harmful environments.
Reflections & Growth
Working on SHADE emphasized the critical importance of designing inclusive spaces tailored specifically for teenagers. Facing practical challenges such as material selection, weight management, and waterproofing provided hands-on experience in iterative problem-solving, while also underscoring the significance of community input in sustainable and empowering design.
Looking Ahead
Future iterations will include exploring magnetic and chalk wall integrations, refining our can wall design, and expanding our soft wall installations. These enhancements will continue to prioritize youth engagement, adaptability, and community involvement, further strengthening the project's long-term sustainability and positive social impact.
